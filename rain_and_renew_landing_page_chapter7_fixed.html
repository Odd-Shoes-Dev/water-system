02 / A practical answer

Now bring the water closer.

Rain falls on every roof. A tank can keep it for the days when it doesn't.

Rain&Renew installs rainwater harvesting systems at households in water-insecure communities. The first two pilot tanks hold 2,000 litres and 1,000 litres.

Water used for washing doesn't have to be wasted either. Treated greywater can be reused for non-drinking purposes, so every drop works twice.

04 / The value of every drop

Water saved is value returned.

Greywater reuse can reduce demand for fresh water. A published household model gives us one way to understand the potential—without confusing estimates with Rain&Renew's pilot results.

Estimated simple payback from water-bill savings alone, using the study's assumptions. This is a long payback period, so the case for reuse should not be presented as a quick financial return.

Why reuse can still matter

Beyond household bills, safely treated greywater can help conserve freshwater, support non-drinking uses and improve resilience when water is scarce.

06 / Beyond the household

Then look to the river.

The River Rwizi serves Mbarara and the communities around it. The River Rwizi catchment faces pressures commonly associated with wetland encroachment, siltation and changing land use. Protecting the river will require local evidence, community participation and coordination with relevant authorities. Catchment pressures include wetland encroachment, siltation and land-use changes.

08 / The person behind the pilot

It started with one person and two tanks.

Arinda Catherine is a lawyer by profession and an award-winning youth leader by passion, working at the forefront of social and environmental justice.

She specialises in turning legal frameworks into grassroots action, using the law as a catalyst for social and environmental reform.

As the visionary behind the HerRights Project, recognised as the Best Community Project, she spearheaded the enactment of a sub-county bylaw to end child marriage and promote education in Rubindi.

She is now applying her legal expertise and policy advocacy to advance youth-led action addressing ecological and climate challenges in western Uganda.

Why there is no price list yet

Current indicative costs per household are about UGX 1.5 million for a 2,000-litre rainwater harvesting system and about UGX 1.8 million for a greywater recycling system. The right mix differs for every household, and schools and hospitals need separate assessment. Indicative costs; actual costs depend on household needs and technical assessment.

The full budget will also cover training, installation differences, maintenance, monitoring, river activities and staffing. We will publish it before asking for a specific amount, and we will never claim a donation covers more than it does.

Are the results verified?

The figures are reported by Cathy and are approximate. Each is shown with its reporting period, and litres are cumulative totals, not stored water.

Is the recycled water safe?

Greywater is treated and reused only for non-drinking purposes. Systems are designed and maintained with health and safety in mind.

Is Rain&Renew part of YFC?

No. Cathy works with YFC, and that experience informs her work, but Rain&Renew is her own initiative. Rain&Renew is presented as Cathy’s own initiative; any formal partnerships should be described accurately.

Has the river programme started?

Not yet. The reporting tool and clean-ups are planned for the next phase, and we label them that way everywhere.
